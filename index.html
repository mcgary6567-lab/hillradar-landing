Congress stock trades, explained

A short primer on the public records behind this site, for anyone searching for senator or representative stock trades for the first time.

What is the STOCK Act?

The Stop Trading on Congressional Knowledge Act of 2012 requires US senators and representatives, and their spouses and dependent children, to publicly disclose stock, bond, option and fund transactions over $1,000 within 45 days. Those Periodic Transaction Reports are the source of every row on Hillradar AI.

What a congressional trade filing contains

The lawmaker, the company or asset and its ticker, whether it was a purchase, sale or exchange, the trade date, the filing date and an amount range such as $15,001–$50,000. Filings never state exact dollar amounts, which is why trackers show bands.

Questions

Where does the data come from?

From the public disclosures members of Congress must file under the STOCK Act with the Clerk of the House and the Secretary of the Senate, as published in open datasets built from those filings, plus the official congressional directory for names, parties and photos. Every trade traces back to an official filing.

Is this legal and is it affiliated with Congress?

Yes, it is legal: these records are published precisely so the public can read them. Hillradar is independent and not affiliated with any government body, party or lawmaker.

Can members of Congress legally trade stocks?

Yes. US senators and representatives are allowed to buy and sell stocks, but the STOCK Act requires them to publicly disclose each transaction over $1,000 within 45 days. Hillradar collects those disclosures so anyone can read them.

Is congressional stock trading insider trading?

No. The trades Hillradar tracks are the legal, public disclosures the STOCK Act requires. Trading on material non-public information would be illegal insider trading for anyone, in or out of Congress, but the filings here are the public record the law created so the public can watch for conflicts of interest.

Are congressional stock trades public?

Yes. Every trade is filed as a public Periodic Transaction Report with the Clerk of the House or the Secretary of the Senate. Hillradar turns those filings into a searchable database, updated daily.

How do I track a specific member of Congress, like Nancy Pelosi?

Search any senator or representative by name to see their full disclosed trading record, then follow them to get a Telegram, WhatsApp or email alert the moment they file a new trade.

How fast do new filings appear?

Usually within hours of publication. Note that the law gives lawmakers up to 45 days to file after a trade; we show that delay on every record and mark late filings.
